# Sending a Caller to Voicemail

Sometimes a call comes in and nobody on your team can take it right now. You're mid-conversation with
someone else, you're the only person on shift, or the caller simply needs to leave a message rather
than wait.

**Send to Voicemail** lets you offer that caller the voicemail greeting straight away, instead of
leaving them on hold listening to music.

:::warning This ends the call for your whole team
This is **not** a personal "not me" button. When you send a caller to voicemail, they leave the queue
entirely — the ringing task disappears for **everyone**, and no one else can pick it up.

That's the point of the feature. Just be sure it's what you want before you confirm.
:::

## When to use it

| Use it when | Use something else when |
|---|---|
| Nobody is free and the caller is better served leaving a message | A teammate is available — use the **red X** instead, which passes the call along |
| You're the only one on shift and already on a call | You just don't want *this* call — the red X is the right button |
| The caller would rather leave details than hold | You want to keep the caller waiting for a person — do nothing; it keeps ringing |

**The difference in one line:** the red X passes the call to the next agent. Send to Voicemail takes
the caller out of the queue and gives them the greeting.

## How to do it

**1. Find the button on the ringing call.**

It sits to the right of the usual green accept and red decline buttons, with a voicemail icon. Hover
over it and it tells you what it is.

**2. Click it, and read the confirmation.**

Connie asks you to confirm, and spells out what's about to happen. Nothing has happened yet at this
point — **Keep it ringing** backs out with no harm done.

**3. Choose **Send to voicemail**.**

The caller hears your organization's voicemail greeting and can leave a message.

## What happens next

The caller's message comes back to your team as a **voicemail task** in the queue, the same as any
voicemail. It carries the caller's number and, if Connie recognises them, their name and profile — so
whoever picks it up already knows who called.

:::info It waits four full days
A voicemail task stays in your queue for **96 hours**, not one hour. A message left late on Friday is
still waiting on Monday morning. See
**[How Long a Task Stays in Your Queue](/end-users/staff-agents/handling-tasks/how-long-tasks-last)**.
:::

Your reporting shows this correctly too. A caller you sent to voicemail is **not** counted as an
abandoned call — it's recorded as a deliberate choice you made, and the voicemail is linked back to
the original call.

## If something doesn't look right

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **The button isn't there** | It only appears on a **ringing** voice call. Once you've accepted a call, or for emails, faxes and chats, it isn't offered. If a ringing call is missing it entirely, your organization may not have it switched on yet — ask your administrator. |
| **"That call is no longer waiting"** | A teammate accepted it, or the caller hung up, in the moment between your click and your confirm. Nothing went wrong and nothing was lost. |
| **The button is greyed out briefly** | It's working. It goes back to normal once the caller has been moved. |

## Questions people ask

**Can I undo it?**
No. Once the caller has been sent to voicemail, they're out of the queue. That's why Connie asks you
to confirm first.

**Will my teammates know it was me?**
Yes — the action is recorded against your name. This isn't a way to quietly clear a call, and it
isn't meant to be.

**Does the caller know they were sent there by a person?**
No. They simply hear the voicemail greeting, the same as if they'd chosen it themselves or called
outside your hours.

**Can I still see the message afterwards?**
Yes. It comes back to the team queue as a voicemail task like any other — you or a colleague can pick
it up, listen, read the transcript, and follow up.
